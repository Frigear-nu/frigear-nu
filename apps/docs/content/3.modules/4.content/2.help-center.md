---
title: Help center
description: Bilingual help articles in categories, public or visible per role, shown as article lists, FAQ accordions or FAQ cards, with onboarding checklists that track what each volunteer has read, from the database or Nuxt Content.
---

The help center holds articles that explain how things work: how to get to the site, what to do at the bar, what a
shift lead expects. [Staff](/guide/concepts/glossary) write them in both languages under **Admin → Help center**, file
them in categories and decide who may read each one. Anybody reads the public ones at `/help`. Signed-in volunteers read
the rest at `/account/help`, where onboarding checklists tick off the articles they have read, and a coordinator sees who
has not finished.

The package is `@frigear-nu/help-center`. Who may read a category, an article or a checklist, and who a send reaches, is
a grant list (Auth's [record audiences](/contribute/writing-a-module/access-control#record-audiences)); owners follow the [volunteering contracts](/modules/organisation/teams#owners).
Every category is of one [kind](#help-and-faq-categories): `help` (the help center's own articles) or `faq` (short
questions and answers embedded on the site's pages). A category is shown as an [accordion](#faq-accordion-categories),
whose articles open in place, or as [cards](#faq-cards-categories), where each article is a card of its own; the FAQ
components show them on any page.

## What you get

- **Two areas, one API.** A public area (`/help`) that shows public articles only, the same for every visitor and
  therefore cacheable and indexable, and a signed-in area (`/account/help`) with the articles the person is granted,
  checklists with read state and "recently updated for you". Both paths are options.
- **Articles in categories**, in Danish and English, as Markdown (MDC when Nuxt Content is installed). A category carries
  who may read it; an article can only narrow it.
- **FAQ accordions and cards.** A category shown as an `accordion` lists its articles as questions that open in place;
  one shown as `cards` makes each article a card with its own questions and buttons. Both carry `FAQPage` JSON-LD for
  search engines. The global `Faq`, `FaqCards`, `FaqGrid`, `FaqSectionList` and `FaqCard` components show such
  categories, or cards written in page frontmatter, in Markdown and on custom pages.
- **Onboarding checklists.** A list of articles per everyone or per operation. Opening an article marks it as read. A
  coordinator reads the progress table.
- **Article sources.** The database (edited in admin) and [Nuxt Content](https://content.nuxt.com) (files in git), merged
  by slug, behind one interface that a host can add to.
- **Sending.** Links to articles by email and SMS, to the people each article is allowed for.
- **Search** over title and text in both languages, shared by every source.
- **Admin area** (opt-in, with [Admin](/modules/interface/admin)): articles, categories and checklists in tabs, a
  checklist page with its progress table, and an FAQ page with the `faq` categories and their questions. Neither shows
  the other's records.
- **Account integration**: an account menu item and a "Your onboarding" widget on the account overview.
- **A mobile Help tab** with [Mobile](/modules/interface/mobile).
- **Composables and helpers**: `useHelpCenterPaths`, `useHelpCenterArticles`, `useHelpCenterSearch`,
  `useHelpCenterFaqs`, `toFaqCardProps`, `HelpCenterSignedInCta`, the path builders `helpCenterApi` and
  `createHelpCenterPaths`, `defineHelpCenterSource`.
- **Emails.** One template, `help-center-articles`, for [Email templates](/modules/communication/email-templates) when
  installed.

## Quick setup

::steps

### Install the help center

```bash
pnpm add @frigear-nu/help-center @nuxthub/core
```

### Add it to nuxt.config.ts

List Admin (and Menu, Account) before the help center, so the pages and the menu entries register. The two areas are on
by default; the admin pages are opt-in.

```ts [nuxt.config.ts]
export default defineNuxtConfig({
  modules: ['@nuxthub/core', '@frigear-nu/admin', '@frigear-nu/help-center'],
  helpCenter: {
    settings: {
      pages: { admin: true },
    },
  },
})
```

### Generate the migration

:::warning
Without a [migration](/guide/concepts/glossary) the `help_categories`, `help_articles`, `help_checklists`,
`help_checklist_items` and `help_reads` tables do not exist, and every request to them fails.
:::

```bash
pnpm db:generate
```

### Decide who manages the help center

:::warning
Staff routes check permissions through [Authorization](/modules/identity/authorization). Without a role that holds
`help-center:manage`, nobody can manage articles. The `admin` role holds it, and `help-center:send` and
`help-center:pii`, when your app defines it. Grant them to your own roles by name
(`authorization.roles.<role>.grant: ['help-center:manage', 'help-center:send']`).
:::

```ts [nuxt.config.ts]
export default defineNuxtConfig({
  authorization: {
    roles: {
      editor: { label: 'Editor', grant: ['help-center:manage'], inherit: ['user'] },
    },
  },
})
```

::

::callout{color="success" icon="i-lucide-check"}
**What you should see:** a signed-in coordinator opens `/admin/help-center`, adds a category and an article in Danish and
English, publishes it and sets the category to public. `/help` then lists the category to everyone, and a volunteer
finds the same article at `/account/help`.
::

## Options

Options go under the `helpCenter` key, in `settings`.

| Option | Type | Default | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `settings.pages.public` | `string \| false` | `'/help'` | The public area. `false` turns it off. Category and article pages follow below it. |
| `settings.pages.signedIn` | `string \| false` | `'/account/help'` | The signed-in area. `false` turns it off. |
| `settings.pages.admin` | `string \| boolean` | off | The staff pages. `true` is `/admin/help-center`. Needs Admin. |
| `settings.requiredLocales` | `string[]` | `['da', 'en']` | Languages an article needs in title and body before it can be published. |
| `settings.cta` | `boolean` | `true` | Show `HelpCenterSignedInCta`. `false` hides it everywhere the module places it. |
| `settings.accountMenu` | `boolean` | `true` | List the signed-in area in the account menu (when it is under `/account`). |
| `settings.accountWidget` | `boolean` | `true` | Add "Your onboarding" to `account.overview`. |
| `settings.signedInLayout` | `string` | none | A layout for a signed-in area outside `/account`. |
| `settings.signedInMenu` | `string` | none | The menu to list a signed-in area outside `/account` in. |
| `settings.sources` | `string[]` | `['database']` | The article sources, in merge order. `'content'` is added when `content.collection` is set. |
| `settings.content.collection` | `string` | none | The Nuxt Content collection that holds articles. See [Nuxt Content](#nuxt-content). |
| `settings.content.perLocale` | `boolean` | `false` | One collection per language (`help_da`, `help_en`) instead of a `locale` field. |
| `settings.content.precedence` | `string` | `'database'` | The id of the source (`database`, `content` or a registered one) that wins when two articles share a slug. Categories always put the database first. |
| `settings.content.missingLocales` | `'warn' \| 'error'` | `'warn'` | What a content article missing a required language does at build time. |
| `settings.mobileTab` | `boolean` | `true` | Add a Help tab to the mobile app. Needs Mobile. |
| `settings.schema` | `boolean` | `true` | Emit `Article` JSON-LD for public articles and `FAQPage` JSON-LD for public accordion and cards categories. |
| `settings.faq.contactComponent` | `string` | none | Name of a globally resolvable component an FAQ card renders for its `contact`. See [The contact button](#the-contact-button). |
| `settings.faq.contactForm` | `string` | `'contact'` | Slug of the Forms form the built-in contact button opens. |

::note
The resolved paths are in public runtime config, so the mobile app and emails build the same links.
::

## The two areas

Both areas use the same API and the same components; only the reader differs.

| Area | Default path | Who sees what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | `/help`, `/help/:category`, `/help/:category/:slug` | **Public articles only**, for every visitor, signed in or not. The page asks for `?public=1`, never reads the session, and is identical for everyone, so it caches and indexes the same way. |
| Signed in | `/account/help`, `/account/help/:category`, `/account/help/:category/:slug` | Public and restricted articles the person is granted, onboarding checklists with read state, "recently updated for you". |

- **Public pages** are a site page: `UContainer > UPage > UPageHeader`, inside the host's layout. A restricted category
  or article answers "not found" on `/help`, also to a signed-in person who could read it in the signed-in area.
- **Signed-in pages** are app pages: `PageHeading`, in the account layout. Opening an article there (or in the public
  area, when signed in) marks it as read on the client.
- **The button into the signed-in area.** `HelpCenterSignedInCta` shows signed-in people a "Crew help center" button in the
  header of `/help` and at the end of each public article. It renders on the client only, so the public page stays the
  same for every visitor. Place it anywhere else (site header, footer, account menu) or hide it with `cta: false`.
- **Accordion categories.** A category page shows an `accordion` category's articles as questions that open in place,
  in the public area as what visitors may read, in the signed-in area as what the person may read, and a `cards`
  category's articles as a grid of cards. See [FAQ accordion categories](#faq-accordion-categories) and
  [FAQ cards categories](#faq-cards-categories).
- **FAQ components inside an article.** In the public area an article's `::faq`, `::faq-grid` and `::faq-section-list`
  render with `public` forced on, whoever looks, so `/help` is the same for every visitor and a signed-in person does not
  get a restricted question embedded in a public article. In the signed-in area they ask as the person.
- **An article's address** is `<base>/<its category>/<slug>`. A request with another category in the path is redirected
  (`301`) to the real one.

### Move or turn off an area

```ts [nuxt.config.ts]
export default defineNuxtConfig({
  helpCenter: {
    settings: {
      pages: {
        public: '/hjaelp', // or false for a host that keeps everything internal
        signedIn: '/crew/help',
      },
      // Outside /account the account layout and menu do not reach: say where it goes.
      signedInLayout: 'crew',
      signedInMenu: 'crew',
    },
  },
})
```

- A path starts with `/`, has no query or hash, is not the site root, and is neither shared by the two areas nor inside
  the other one (the pages of an outer area would swallow the inner area's); otherwise the build fails with a message
  naming the option.
- **Outside `/account`** the signed-in area gets an `authenticated` route rule under every locale prefix
  ([`guardPages`](/modules/identity/authorization/client#guard-a-modules-pages), enforced by
  [Authorization](/modules/identity/authorization), also on the server) and, with `signedInLayout`, your layout. It has no
  account menu item: list it in `signedInMenu` instead.
- **Under `/account`** the [Account](/modules/identity/account) module guards and frames it, and the account menu item and
  the overview widget are on (`accountMenu`, `accountWidget`).
- **Links.** Every link is built from the configured paths through `createHelpCenterPaths` (server, emails) or
  `useHelpCenterPaths()` (pages, which also keep the locale prefix). No component hard-codes `/help`.

```ts
const paths = useHelpCenterPaths()
paths.public.article('safety', 'first-aid') // '/help/safety/first-aid'
paths.signedIn.index() // '/account/help', or undefined when the area is off
```

## Write articles

An article belongs to one category, has a unique URL name, a title and a body in each language, and starts as a
**draft**. The body is Markdown, MDC when your app has Nuxt Content, so `::faq{category="membership"}` works inside it.

Publishing needs text in every language of `requiredLocales` (`da` and `en` by default) in both the title and the body;
otherwise the route answers `409` with the code `missing_translations`, and `data.missing` lists what is lacking:

```json
{ "missing": [{ "field": "title", "locales": ["da"] }] }
```

A plain string counts as no language: it is one text for every language, and the rule exists to make sure each language
was written on purpose. A published article keeps the rule: an edit that removes a language is refused the same way. The
editor shows what an article still lacks while you type. Only `published` articles reach the public API, whoever asks.

In the admin, **Publish** in an article's menu stays enabled. On an article that still lacks a language, the item names
what is missing and opens the edit slideover with those fields marked; the slideover of a draft has its own **Publish
article** button, enabled once every required language is filled in (it saves, then publishes). A refused publish lists
`data.missing` in its toast.

**A reader whose language an article lacks** sees it in another one, never blank: the pages show the reader's language,
then the site's default locale, then English, then any language the article has. With `requiredLocales: ['da']`, a Danish
article reads in Danish for an English reader.

## Help and FAQ categories

A category's `kind` says what it is for, and is set when it is created: a `PATCH` may repeat it but not change it
(`400 kind_fixed`). How it is shown (`display`) stays a separate setting.

| Kind | What it holds | Shown as, by default | Where it shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `help` (the default) | The help center's articles. | `list` | The category grid, its own page, article pages, checklists and sends. |
| `faq` | Questions and answers embedded on the site's pages (`::faq{category}`, `FaqSectionList` and the others). | `cards` | Where they are embedded, and the **FAQ** section of the help center's start page. No category or article pages. |

- **Two admin pages, never mixed.** The help center admin works with `help` categories and their articles only, the FAQ
  admin with `faq` ones only: lists, counts, the category and article forms and their pickers, the checklist and send
  pickers. Every admin route takes `?kind=help|faq`, and the pages pass theirs: a list keeps to that kind, and a record of
  the other kind is a `404`. An article stays with its kind: it can only move to a category of the same kind, and a
  parent must be of the same kind too (`400`). An FAQ question cannot be on a checklist (`400`).
- **On `/help`.** The start page lists the `help` categories, then a **Frequently asked questions** section with every
  `faq` category the reader may read, as questions that open in place. Search finds both kinds; an FAQ hit is labelled
  **FAQ** and links to `/help?faq=<question>#faq-<category>`, which scrolls to the category and opens the question
  (`useHelpCenterPaths().public.faq(category, slug)`). The old addresses of an FAQ category or question
  (`/help/<category>`, `/help/<category>/<question>`) answer `301` to that place.
- **Sources.** A source's categories are `help` unless it says otherwise (`kind` on `HelpSourceCategory`); a Nuxt Content
  article is always `help`. The merged DTOs carry `kind` on every category and article.

## FAQ accordion categories

Short questions and answers, such as "Membership" or "Volunteering", are usually a `faq` category whose **Shown as** is
**Questions (FAQ)** (`display: 'accordion'`); a `help` category can be shown that way too. Each article is a question: its title is the question, its body the answer.
Everything else stays the same: who may read it, drafts and publishing, search, checklists and article pages.

- **On the category page** the questions open in place instead of linking to article pages. In the public area the
  questions are added to the page's `FAQPage` JSON-LD; the signed-in area has none.
- **An icon and buttons.** In an accordion category an article can carry an `icon`, shown next to the question, and up to
  ten `links` (`{ label, to, icon? }`), shown as buttons under the answer. A button goes to a path on the site, an
  `https://` address, a `mailto:` or `tel:` link or an anchor; anything else is a `400`. The category's own `icon` is the
  card's icon.
- **Order.** **Order articles** in a category's row menu opens a list with a drag handle and up/down buttons. Each move is
  saved at once through `POST /api/admin/help-center/categories/:id/reorder`, which takes every article of the category
  (drafts too) exactly once, or answers `400 invalid_order`. Nothing moves while a save runs. The update goes in
  statements of at most 90 articles (D1's limit of 100 bound parameters), so past 90 articles a failed statement can
  leave part of the new order.

## FAQ cards categories

**Shown as** **Question cards (FAQ)** (`display: 'cards'`) makes each article of the category a card of its own, in a grid:
the article's title and icon head the card, and its text is split into the parts of the card by three blocks. Everything
else is as in an accordion category (grants, drafts, order, search, the `FAQPage` JSON-LD with the whole text as the
answer). The help center admin's article form shows the syntax under the text field of an FAQ category.

```md [article text]
Application → General assembly → Review → Reply.

::faq-details{label="More about the process..." icon="i-lucide-check-circle"}
- ✅ Application submitted no later than 10 days before the general assembly.
- ✅ The general assembly is held in Q1. :br The date is published 3 weeks before.
::

::faq-link{label="Become a member" to="/en/membership" icon="i-lucide-user-plus" trailing-icon="i-lucide-arrow-right" variant="subtle" size="lg"}
::

::faq-contact{label="Contact us" :initial='{"subject":"other"}'}
::
```

| Part | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| The Markdown outside the blocks | The card's description. |
| `::faq-details{label icon}` | A question that opens in the card: `label` is its button, each item of its list a line (no bullets; `:br` breaks a line). |
| `::faq-link{label to icon trailing-icon variant size color target}` | A full-width button at the foot of the card, after the questions. The label can also be the block's content. |
| `::faq-contact{label :initial}` | The card's [contact button](#the-contact-button), with `initial` (JSON) for the form. |

The article's `links` follow the buttons of its text. Each language has its own text, so labels, paths and icons can
differ per language. The text is split with `parseMarkdown` of `@nuxtjs/mdc` when the FAQ is fetched (Nuxt Content brings
it), so the cards render on the server; without it the whole text is the description. The blocks are global components
(`FaqDetails`, `FaqLink`, `FaqContact`) that also render on their own, so the same text reads well on the article's page
and in an accordion category: the details as a collapsible, the link as a button.

### Show questions in Markdown

The components are global, so a content page uses them in MDC without importing anything. A category is named by its URL
name (`slug`); one the caller may not read, or one that does not exist, renders nothing.

```md [content/en/pages/membership.md]
::faq{category="membership"}
::

::faq-grid{categories="membership,volunteering"}
::

::faq-section-list{categories="volunteering"}
::
```

| Component | Takes | Renders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Faq` | `category` (a slug), `contact`, `public` | One category as a card of questions (a card per article for a `cards` category). |
| `FaqCards` | The same as `FaqGrid` | The cards alone, without a wrapper, for a grid the parent lays out, such as the `#features` slot of `UPageSection`. |
| `FaqGrid` | `categories` (slugs, comma separated or an array), `items`, `contact`, `public`, `loading` | The cards in a grid. |
| `FaqSectionList` | The same as `FaqGrid` | A page section's FAQ at the width it gets: one card as its questions at reading width, without a card frame; several in a grid that fills the width (container queries, so it fits an article too). |
| `FaqCard` | `title`, `description`, `icon`, `to`, `faqs`, `links`, `contact`, `public` | One card, from props. Answers render through `HelpCenterArticleBody`, so with `public` an FAQ component inside an answer asks as a visitor too. |

`FaqGrid` and `FaqSectionList` also take inline `items` (`FaqCardProps[]`), such as cards kept in a page's frontmatter;
the categories come first, then the inline cards. Inline cards are not stored anywhere and have no grants: anything that
should be edited by staff, translated in the editor or shown to some people only belongs in a category.

```md [content/en/pages/board.md]
::faq-grid{categories="board" :items='meta.cards'}
::
```

```yaml [frontmatter]
cards:
  - title: Who is on the board?
    icon: i-lucide-info
    faqs:
      - title: Is it paid?
        details: No, the board is 100% volunteer.
```

Each question opens as a collapsible: the question is the button, the answer a quote below it, and the buttons follow the
answer. Every component adds the public questions of its categories to the page's one `FAQPage` JSON-LD block, listed
once however many components show them, with the answers as plain text. `settings.schema: false` turns it off.

### Show questions on a custom page

`useHelpCenterFaqs` fetches categories with their published articles and text, and `toFaqCardProps` turns one into the
props of `FaqCard` in the current language.

```vue [app/pages/volunteers.vue]
<script setup lang="ts">
const { faqs, status } = await useHelpCenterFaqs(['volunteering', 'membership'])
</script>

<template>
  <FaqGrid
    :items="faqs.map(toFaqCardProps)"
    :loading="status === 'pending'"
  />
</template>
```

`useHelpCenterFaqs(categories?, options?)` takes a slug, an array of slugs or a comma separated list (none means every
`faq` category the caller may read), and `{ public?, lazy? }`. It returns
`{ faqs, articles, parts, status, error, refresh }` (`parts`: the texts of `cards` categories split into their cards),
built on `useAsyncData`, so it works with server rendering. Its fetch key includes the signed-in user, so what a
restricted category showed to one person is never reused for the next one. With `public: true` it asks for what visitors
read only, and the response is cacheable. Each entry of `faqs` is a category (`HelpFaqDto`) with its `articles`, text
included.

### The contact button

A card's `contact` prop (`{ label, initial }`) adds a button at the bottom of the card. What it opens is decided by the
host:

1. **`settings.faq.contactComponent`**: the name of a component the card renders instead, with the card's `label` and
   `initial`. It must be resolvable by name, so register it globally (a component in `components/global/`, or
   `global: true` in `addComponent`).
2. **Forms installed, no `contactComponent`**: a built-in button opens a modal with the form of `settings.faq.contactForm`
   (`contact` by default). It does not prefill the form; use a `contactComponent` for that.
3. **Neither**: no button.

```ts [nuxt.config.ts]
export default defineNuxtConfig({
  helpCenter: { settings: { faq: { contactComponent: 'ContactFormModal' } } },
})
```

## Who sees what

Every category, article and checklist stores who may read it as a grant list (`grants`, Auth's
[record audiences](/contribute/writing-a-module/access-control#record-audiences)). Any one grant admits, and an empty list admits nobody: it is never "every signed-in
person" (that is a `signed-in` grant). There is no separate `public` column: a category is public when it holds a
`public` grant.

| Grant | Who reads it |
| --- | --- |
| `{ kind: 'public' }` | Everyone, without signing in. |
| `{ kind: 'signed-in' }` | Everyone who is signed in. A send to it reaches nobody: it lists no members. |
| `{ kind: 'role', role }` | Signed-in people holding that global role (with [Authorization](/modules/identity/authorization#role-grants)). |
| `{ kind: 'team', teamId, minRole }` | Signed-in people with at least that role on the team, through the team kind's ladder (with [Teams](/modules/organisation/teams)). |
| `{ kind: 'team', teamKind, minRole }` | The same on any team of that kind (with [Teams](/modules/organisation/teams#grants)). |
| `{ kind: 'operation', operationId }` | Signed-in people who take part in the operation (with [Operations](/modules/volunteering/operations) and crew). |
| `[]` | Nobody. Staff still see it in the admin area. |

A new category starts with `[]`. To show a category to every signed-in person, add a `signed-in` grant.

A category's grants apply to all its articles, and an article can only narrow them. An article with `grants: null`
inherits its category; an article with a list is read only by people who read the category and match the list. So a
public article in a restricted category stays hidden from visitors, and a role-only article in a public category hides
from everyone without the role.

**Subcategories inherit what their ancestors hide.** A category is readable only when it and every category above it are,
so a subcategory can narrow its parent and never widen it, and an article inherits the whole chain. That holds in the
listings, by an article's address, in search, in checklists, when marking an article as read and when sending links. A
public subcategory below a restricted parent is therefore hidden from visitors, and is not `public` in the DTOs.

- **Through a permission.** Every record is read through `grantsCanRead(event, 'help-center:read', record)`: a `public`
  grant needs no sign-in, anything else goes through `help-center:read` and its policy, which evaluates the record's own
  grants. The answer is cached per record for the request. A send checks each recipient's own grants instead.
- **Checked on write.** The body takes the shape of a list; the handler checks it with `grantsValidate`, so an unknown
  kind, an invalid grant or an operation or team that does not exist is a `400`. A grant that is already stored is not
  checked again. A stored grant of a kind no installed module knows admits nobody.
- **Owners.** A category belongs to the platform (the default) or to an operation or team, checked through Teams'
  `teams:owner:resolve` hook.
- **Unreadable is 404**, the same answer as for an article that does not exist.

## Sources

Articles come from one or more sources behind one interface, so the API, grants, search, checklists and read state work
the same for all of them. Sources are merged by slug: the first with a slug wins, and `content.precedence` puts one first.

```ts
type HelpCenterSource = {
  id: string
  readOnly: boolean
  categories(ctx): HelpSourceCategory[] // every category it knows, with whether this reader may read it
  list(ctx): HelpArticleDto[] // published articles the reader may read; ctx has q and category
  get(slug, ctx): HelpArticleDto | undefined
  getById?(id, ctx): HelpArticleDto | undefined // read state and checklists store ids
}
```

- **`database`** is the tables, edited in admin. A category hidden from the reader still claims its slug, so a category a
  file source derives cannot take its place. This holds whatever `content.precedence` says: the precedence decides
  **articles** only, and categories always put the database first.
- **A hidden database article does not claim its slug.** A draft, or an article the reader may not read, answers nothing
  for that slug, so another source's article with the same slug is shown to that reader instead. Articles are looked up
  by id as well: a checklist item or a read of an article that lost a clash still resolves by its own id, but its link
  uses its slug, which opens the winning article.
- **`content`** is Nuxt Content, below. Its articles are always public.
- **Your own.** Define one, register it in a server plugin and list its id:

```ts [server/plugins/wiki-source.ts]
import { defineHelpCenterSource } from '@frigear-nu/help-center/source'

export default defineNitroPlugin(() => {
  registerHelpCenterSource(defineHelpCenterSource({
    id: 'wiki',
    readOnly: true,
    categories: () => [],
    list: () => [],
    get: () => undefined,
  }))
})
```

```ts [nuxt.config.ts]
export default defineNuxtConfig({ helpCenter: { settings: { sources: ['database', 'wiki'] } } })
```

An article of a restricted database category that a source returns stays reachable by its address, because a source says
who may read an article, but the category's listing hides it, so it does not show under the restricted category.

## Nuxt Content

Public articles can live as Markdown files in git, reviewed like code. **They are always public:** Nuxt Content serves
every collection to the browser (`/__nuxt_content/<collection>/sql_dump.txt`), so anything in a collection is effectively
public. Restricted articles come only from the database.

```ts [content.config.ts]
import { defineCollection, defineContentConfig, z } from '@nuxt/content'

export default defineContentConfig({
  collections: {
    help: defineCollection({
      type: 'page', // keeps the default page schema: the article text is read from `rawbody`
      source: { include: 'help/**/*.md', prefix: '/' },
      schema: z.object({
        category: z.string(),
        slug: z.string().optional(),
        order: z.number().optional(),
        checklist: z.union([z.string(), z.array(z.string())]).optional(),
        locale: z.string(),
      }),
    }),
  },
})
```

```ts [nuxt.config.ts]
export default defineNuxtConfig({
  modules: ['@nuxt/content', '@nuxthub/core', '@frigear-nu/help-center'],
  helpCenter: { settings: { content: { collection: 'help', precedence: 'database' } } },
})
```

Collections come from the host's `content.config.ts`, not from a Nuxt option. Put the same file in each language in a
directory named after the language (`content/help/da/safety/first-aid.md`, `content/help/en/safety/first-aid.md`), with
`locale` in the frontmatter, or define one collection per language (`help_da`, `help_en`) and set
`content.perLocale`.

```md [content/help/da/safety/first-aid.md]
---
title: Førstehjælp
locale: da
category: safety
order: 2
checklist: hck_onboarding
---

Ring 112 ved alvorlig skade.
```

| Frontmatter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `title` | The title in that language. Required. |
| `category` | The URL name of the category. Required. A database category with that slug supplies the title, description, grants and FAQ groups; without one the category is named after its slug. |
| `slug` | The URL name. Defaults to the last segment of the path. |
| `order` | Sort order within the category. |
| `checklist` | The id of a checklist (or a list of ids) the article is added to, as a required item. |
| `locale` | The language of the file. Required unless `perLocale` is on. |

- **The id** of a content article is `content:<collection>:<path>`, the path without the language directory, so one article in
  two languages has one id, and `help_reads` and checklist items can point at it.
- **The build fails** on a file that sets `visibility`, `public` or `grants` (content is public), has no `category` or `title`, has
  an unusable `slug`, or lacks `locale` when one collection holds every language. It checks the parsed fields, the `meta`
  Nuxt Content keeps and the raw frontmatter, so a key the schema drops is still caught. A required language missing from
  an article is a build warning, or a failure with `content.missingLocales: 'error'`.
- **In a restricted category.** A content article filed under a restricted database category stays public: the category's
  listing hides it, and it can still be opened by its address. The build cannot warn about the mismatch, because it
  does not know the database's categories; the admin list shows each article's category, which is where to notice it.
- **Admin** shows content articles read-only with a "Source: Nuxt Content" badge. Edit the file instead (admin does not link to it).

## Onboarding checklists

A checklist is an ordered list of articles, marked required or optional, for everybody or for one operation.

- **For people.** `GET /api/help-center/me/checklists` returns the checklists a signed-in person may see: their own
  `grants`, and, when the checklist has an `operationId`, taking part in that operation (the `operation` grant kind;
  without Operations nobody takes part). Each lists only the articles
  the person may read (the others are left out, as if not there) with `read`, `readAt` and the counts. A checklist with
  nothing readable is left out. The "Your onboarding" widget and the signed-in home show them.
- **Reading.** `POST /api/help-center/me/reads` with `{ articleId }` marks an article as read, once (the first time is kept).
  The article must be one the person may read; anything else is the same `404`. The article pages call it when a signed-in
  person opens them. Ids are `hlp_...` for the database, `content:...` for Nuxt Content, whatever a custom source uses.
- **The builder.** A checklist's page (`<admin>/checklists/:id`) edits its articles in order, with a required switch. The
  list is saved as a whole (`PATCH` with `items`), and an article that is not found, or listed twice, is a `400`.
- **Content files** add themselves with the `checklist` frontmatter, as required items after the builder's.

### Who has read what

`GET /api/admin/help-center/checklists/:id/progress` needs **`help-center:pii`**, which no wildcard covers, because which
articles a person read is personal data. It lists people with how many required articles they read, the ones who have not
finished first, with `q` over name and email, sorting and pages. A checklist for an operation also lists the people who take
part in it (the members of its `operation` grant), with nothing read yet, which is how a
coordinator sees who has not started.

### Dashboard item

With [Dashboard](/modules/interface/dashboard) installed, the same summary is also the item `help-center.onboarding` of
`crew.home` (permission `help-center:read`, order 70), next to the `account.overview` widget, which stays. It shows nothing
for people without a checklist. The label is `helpCenter.dashboard.onboarding`.

## Send articles

`POST /api/admin/help-center/send` (needs `help-center:send`) sends links by email, SMS or both:

```json
{
  "articleIds": ["hlp_..."],
  "audience": { "grants": [{ "kind": "operation", "operationId": "opr_..." }], "userIds": ["12"] },
  "channels": ["sms"],
  "fallbackToEmail": true,
  "locale": "da"
}
```

- **Recipients are resolved on the server.** `userIds` are explicit people. `grants` is a grant list: each kind lists its
  own members (`grantsMembers`), in batches under D1's bound parameter limit: `role` the users Authorization assigned the
  role to, `team` the team's members at or above the role, `operation` the people who take part. The grants are checked
  like on write: an unknown kind or a reference that does not exist is a `400`, and so is `public`, which lists nobody.
  Grants that list nobody are `400 audience_not_resolved`, not "sent to nobody". At most 500 people at a time.
- **Nobody gets a link they cannot open.** Each recipient's own grants are checked, and the message lists only the
  articles they may read. A person with none is skipped (`no_access`).
- **Each person's language.** A person gets the message in their own language (`users.locale` of Users). The send's
  `locale` is for people without one, then the first required locale, then English.
- **Links** use the configured paths: the public area for a public article, the signed-in area otherwise, with the locale
  prefix of the person's language when the site uses prefixed routes. The origin is the configured site URL
  (`NUXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL`, through Auth's `authSiteOrigin`), never a forwarded header the client chose; without one the
  request's own origin is used under `nuxt dev` only, and otherwise the send fails with `500 site_url_missing`.
- **The email** is written in the person's language (`da` or `en`; English for any other): the subject of a message with
  several articles (a single article uses its own title), and the `help-center-articles` template's `subject`,
  `heading`, `greeting` and `intro` props, so with Email templates installed the rendered email is in that language too.
- **Every channel** goes through Notify's [`notifyUser`](/modules/communication/notify#notifying-a-user) on the
  `help-center.send` topic (email and push on by default, SMS offered but off): the email with the `help-center-articles` template (a
  fallback body is always there) to the user's own address, and a push notification that names the articles and opens
  the first one. People choose their channels on the account page of
  [Preferences](/modules/identity/preferences#notification-channels). Pushes reach phones with
  [Push](/modules/communication/push) installed.
- **SMS** goes to the user's `phone` (`users.phone` of Users) when the sender chose SMS **and** the person turned on
  texts for the topic: it is off by default, so a send by SMS reaches only the people who opted in. Nobody is texted
  without a number. A send by SMS alone has no push.
- **Fall back to email** (`fallbackToEmail`, on by default). A person a text cannot reach (no phone number, or SMS off
  for them on `help-center.send`) gets the email instead, with its push; the email still follows their email choice for
  the topic. Off, they get nothing and are listed as skipped. A text that failed is not retried by email, and a send that
  already asks for email needs no fallback.

The answer (`HelpSendResult`) counts the recipients by what they got and lists the ones that got neither an email nor a
text, with why:

```json
{
  "recipients": 4,
  "counts": { "sms": 1, "email": 2, "none": 1 },
  "fallback": 2,
  "pushed": 2,
  "skipped": [{ "userId": "17", "name": "Ann", "reason": "opted_out" }]
}
```

| Reason | Why the person got nothing |
| --- | --- |
| `unknown_user` | No user has the id. |
| `no_access` | They may read none of the articles. |
| `no_phone` | A send by SMS without the fallback, and they have no phone number. |
| `no_sms_consent` | A send by SMS without the fallback, and SMS is off for them on `help-center.send` (it is off until they turn it on). |
| `no_email` | The email (asked for, or the fallback) had no address. |
| `opted_out` | The email (asked for, or the fallback) is off for them on `help-center.send`. |
| `failed` | The channel failed (logged on the server). |

The reason is the one of the last channel tried: the email when it was asked for or fallen back to, the text otherwise.
Someone who got both a text and an email is counted in `sms` and `email`. A push goes with the email and is counted in
`pushed` only.

## Search

`GET /api/help-center/articles?q=` finds published articles whose title or text contains the term in any language, ignoring
case, among the ones the caller may read, in every source (50 at most), and `?category=` limits it to one category,
`?kind=` to one [kind](#help-and-faq-categories). Each hit carries `kind`. The home page of both areas has a search box
(`useHelpCenterSearch`) that labels FAQ hits and links them to the FAQ section.

- **Ignoring case**: the term is lowercased in JavaScript, which knows Danish letters; postgresql lowercases the stored text too; sqlite's `lower()` and `LIKE` only fold ASCII, so the
  stored text is compared with the lowercase and the uppercase term.
- **Batched.** The grants are not in SQL, so matches are read in batches until there are 50 visible hits or none are left.
- **Once per request.** The category tree, each article and each content collection are read once per request, so a send
  or a checklist evaluates grants per person in memory instead of querying per person.
- **`?public=1`** searches what anonymous visitors read.

## Limits

Some reads are bounded on purpose. Each cut is logged as a warning.

| What | Limit | Beyond it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public and signed-in article list | 1000 articles | The list is cut (a warning is logged). |
| Search | 50 hits | The rest is not returned; narrow the term or the category. |
| Rows one read scans | 10 000 | The read stops (a warning is logged). |
| Admin article list | The newest 5000 database articles are sorted and paged in memory | `total` is counted in SQL and stays accurate, but pages past the cap are empty: search or filter. |
| One send | 500 people | `400`: send in parts. |

## Admin

With Admin installed and `pages.admin` set:

| Page | Path | What staff do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Help center | `/admin/help-center` | The `help` categories and articles (`?kind=help`). Tabs for articles, categories and checklists. Articles: search, filter by status, category and source, add, edit, publish, unpublish, send, delete. Categories: a tree with article counts and how each is shown; order a category's articles. |
| Checklist | `/admin/help-center/checklists/:id` | Build the checklist and read its progress table. |
| FAQ | `/admin/help-center/faq` | The `faq` categories, each with its questions: add, edit, publish, order and delete questions, edit a category, add an FAQ category (shown as cards by default). The same routes and forms as the help center page, scoped with `?kind=faq`; its sidebar entry is in the Manage group. |

- **Articles** open a slideover with the title and body in every language (a Markdown preview), the category, the URL
  name and who may read it ("Same as the category" or its own list); in an accordion category also its icon and buttons.
  A new article is a draft; publishing is disabled until every language is filled in (the editor shows what is missing).
- **Categories** open a slideover with name, description, parent, how it is shown (an article list, questions or question cards), icon, owner and who may read it (Grants' `<GrantsEditor>`). A category
  with articles cannot be deleted; its subcategories move up when it is.
- **Content articles** are listed read-only with a source badge.
- **Send** opens a slideover to pick articles, people (Users' [`<UserPicker multiple>`](/modules/identity/users#userpicker) on
  `GET /api/admin/help-center/users`, with "Create user" for people with `users:create`), groups (a grant list without
  `public`), channels and "Fall back to email". After the send it shows the counts (by SMS, by email, got nothing) and
  who got nothing with why, and a toast sums it up.
- **Who may read it**: Grants' `<GrantsEditor>`, with every installed grant kind in its "Add" menu. Checklists and
  sends leave `public` out.

The sidebar entry (`help-center-admin`, in `admin.nav`) shows to people with `help-center:manage`. Hiding it is not access
control: every route checks `help-center:manage` itself.

## Permissions

| Name | Flags | Held by default | What it allows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `help-center:manage` | | `admin` | Create and edit categories, articles and checklists, publish and delete. |
| `help-center:send` | | `admin` | Send article links by email and SMS. |
| `help-center:pii` | sensitive | `admin` | See who read what, and people's emails in the send form's search. Never covered by a wildcard. |
| `help-center:read` | implicit | every signed-in user | Read the articles you are granted, and record what you read. |

- **Scope:** a token may use the named scope `help-center` or `help-center:read` itself. A token without it is treated as
  anonymous on the public routes, and refused `me` routes (`insufficient_scope`).
- **Policy on `help-center:read`.** The record (a category, an article or a checklist) is readable when its own grants
  admit the user; chains are walked one record at a time. Public records need no sign-in.
- **Staff routes** check `manage` (`send`, `pii` for those two). A role that holds `help-center:*` manages and sends, and is
  still refused the progress table.

## Routes

Typed path builders are exported as `helpCenterApi` from `@frigear-nu/help-center/api`.

| Route | Who | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/help-center/categories` | Everyone | The categories the caller may read. `?kind=help\|faq`, `?public=1`. |
| `GET /api/help-center/articles` | Everyone | The articles the caller may read, without their text. `?q=`, `?category=<slug>`, `?kind=help\|faq`, `?public=1`. |
| `GET /api/help-center/articles/:slug` | Everyone | One article with its text and category, or `404`. `?public=1`. |
| `GET /api/help-center/faqs` | Everyone | Categories with their published articles and text, for the FAQ cards. `?categories=a,b` (in that order, any kind; none: every `faq` category), `?public=1`. |
| `GET /api/help-center/me/checklists` | `help-center:read` | The caller's checklists with read state. |
| `POST /api/help-center/me/reads` | `help-center:read` | Body `{ articleId }`: mark a readable article as read. |
| `GET`, `POST /api/admin/help-center/categories` | `help-center:manage` | List (an array, with counts; `?kind=` for one kind) and create (`kind` in the body, `help` by default). |
| `PATCH`, `DELETE /api/admin/help-center/categories/:id` | `help-center:manage` | Edit; delete (`409 category_not_empty` while it has articles). |
| `GET /api/admin/help-center/categories/:id/articles` | `help-center:manage` | Every article of the category, drafts too, in order. |
| `POST /api/admin/help-center/categories/:id/reorder` | `help-center:manage` | Body `{ articleIds }`: every article of the category once, in the new order (`400 invalid_order` otherwise). |
| `GET`, `POST /api/admin/help-center/articles` | `help-center:manage` | Paginated list of every source (`q`, `status`, `category`, `source`, `kind`), and add a draft (with `?kind=`, its category must be of that kind). |
| `GET`, `PATCH`, `DELETE /api/admin/help-center/articles/:id` | `help-center:manage` | Read, edit or delete a database article. `GET` also reads an article of another source by its id (read-only); such ids can hold a `/` (`content:help:safety/first-aid`), and Auth refuses an encoded `%2F` under `/api`, so the path builder `helpCenterApi.admin.article(id)` sends them to `/api/admin/help-center/source-articles/<id>` with the slashes kept. |
| `POST /api/admin/help-center/articles/:id/publish` | `help-center:manage` | Publish. `409 missing_translations` when a required language is missing. |
| `POST /api/admin/help-center/articles/:id/unpublish` | `help-center:manage` | Back to a draft. |
| `GET`, `POST /api/admin/help-center/checklists` | `help-center:manage` | List with items, and create (optionally with `items`). |
| `GET`, `PATCH`, `DELETE /api/admin/help-center/checklists/:id` | `help-center:manage` | Read, edit (`items` replaces the list) or delete. |
| `GET /api/admin/help-center/checklists/:id/progress` | `help-center:pii` | Who read what, paginated. |
| `POST /api/admin/help-center/send` | `help-center:send` | Send article links. Answers the counts and the skipped people with why. |
| `GET /api/admin/help-center/users` | `help-center:send` | `?q=`: people for the send form's picker (20 at most). With `help-center:pii` it matches and answers emails too; without, names only. |

- **`?kind=help|faq` on the admin routes** keeps a list to that kind and answers `404` for a category or article of the
  other kind, read, edited, published, ordered or deleted by id. Without it a route serves both kinds.
- **`?public=1`** ignores who asks: it never reads the session, answers the same to everyone, and sets
  `Cache-Control: public, max-age=60, stale-while-revalidate=300`. The other answers are `private, no-store`.
- **Reserved URL names:** `search`, `categories`, `articles`, `me`, `new`, `edit`, `admin` and `api`.
- **Responses** are allowlisted DTOs with ISO dates. The public DTO has no status, grants or audit data; an article says
  whether it is `public`, and every category and article its `kind`. Create and edit take `slug`, `name`, `description`,
  `parentId`, `owner`, `grants`, `kind` (create only; an edit may repeat it), `display` (`list`, `accordion` or `cards`), `icon` and `sort` for a category, and `categoryId`, `slug`, `title`, `body`, `grants`
  (`null` inherits the category), `icon`, `links` and `sort` for an article. `title` and `body` are a string or a `{ da, en }` map; the status has its own routes.
- **Errors** use the module toolkit's shape: `statusMessage` is the message, `data.code` a stable code
  (`missing_translations`, `category_not_empty`, `invalid_order`, `kind_fixed`, `conflict`, `not_found`, `forbidden`, `insufficient_scope`,
  `not_configured`).

## Database

Tables the module adds. Generate and commit a [migration](/get-started/database#migrations) after installing.

| Table | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `help_categories` | `hlc_` id, a unique `slug`, Translated `name` and `description`, `parentId`, `ownerType` and `ownerId` (`platform` by default, no id), `grants` (JSON, `[]` by default), `kind` (`help` by default, or `faq`; fixed once created), `display` (`list` by default, `accordion` or `cards`), `icon`, `sort` and the dates. |
| `help_articles` | `hlp_` id, `categoryId`, a unique `slug`, Translated `title` and `body`, `searchText` and `searchBody` (title and slug, and body, folded for search), `icon`, `links` (JSON buttons), `status` (`draft` or `published`), `grants` (JSON, `null` inherits the category), `sort`, `publishedAt`, `updatedBy` and the dates. |
| `help_checklists` | `hck_` id, `operationId` (none for everybody), Translated `name`, `grants` (JSON, `[]` by default), `sort` and the dates. |
| `help_checklist_items` | `hci_` id, `checklistId` (deleted with it), `articleId` (a `hlp_` id or `content:...`), `sort`, `required`. |
| `help_reads` | `hlr_` id, `userId`, `articleId` and `readAt`, unique per person and article. |

Translated texts are a string or a `{ da, en }` map. `help_articles.categoryId` is a restricting foreign key, so a category
with articles cannot be deleted. `help_checklist_items.articleId` and `help_reads.articleId` are plain text because they also
hold ids of other sources.

## Translations

Override any string in your app's `i18n/locales/<code>.json`. The module registers `da` and `en` files under
`helpCenter.*`. See [overriding module strings](/modules/platform/module-kit/i18n#overriding-module-strings-in-your-app).
Without `@nuxtjs/i18n`, the pages use `en`.

Key groups: `menu.*`, `permissions.*`, `scopes.*`, `mobile.*`, `cta.*`, `checklist.*`, `widget.*`, `category.*`,
`article.*`, `faq.*` and `admin.*` (with `admin.grants*`, `admin.displays.*` and `admin.ownerTypes.*`).
